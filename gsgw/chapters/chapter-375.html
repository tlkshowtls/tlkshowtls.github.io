CHAPTER 375

And so, a morning as listless and miserable as ever dawned upon me.




— Good morning, everyone. Workers, please get ready for today’s tasks~




I woke up in the cramped workers’ break room, still feeling like a piece of trash, lying next to a corpse.

I’d held out a faint hope that I might be back in my bed at home, but it was a hopeless fantasy.

‘Ugh…’

There’s no way that could be true.

‘As if this ghost story universe would be that easy.’

I splashed some water on my face and got up from the worn-out, sagging mattress.

My body, which had consumed nothing but chocolate, ached in every joint, and my head was spinning. Even in this state, I was struggling not to look at the corpse with its eyes wide open at the far end of the wall.

‘Hah.’

I laid as far from it as possible, brainwashing myself into believing it was just a statue. Despite this, I ended up falling asleep as if I had passed out because my physical condition was just that terrible…

‘Turns out, being in such a terrible shape actually helps.’




— Morning roll call in 10 minutes. Have a prosperous day~




Even the morning announcement sounded like it belonged in some illegal labor dorm for debtors. It was so painfully realistic that it felt like my sanity was about to snap.

And now they expected me to enter a ghost story in this state? It was terrifying, though for a completely different reason than when I was on the Security Team.

‘My days as a full-time employee, when merchandise used to descend from that mysterious black box, feels like a dream now…’

But even in this terrible state, my mind was still working.

And my plan was clear.

‘...All right.’

I took a deep breath and, staggering slightly, stood by the door.



Squeeeek!



A moment later, the door opened.

I immediately scanned the hallway outside.

From every door of the workers’ break rooms lining the hallway, people in shabby uniforms emerged.

...Just as he’d written in his diary, they all had strange, hollow eyes.




[Workers in the Z800 and 900 series, start moving. Do not linger.]




A disinterested employee’s voice issued the instruction over the PA system, and the workers began moving as if they were used to it.

‘Ugh.’

Following them, I gritted my teeth and started walking.

But the moment I got close enough to blend in with the group, several workers openly frowned at me. Some deliberately avoided me, while others spat out curses the instant our eyes met.

"The hell are you staring at, asshole?"

One of them even tried to shove me away with a kick.

Though, for an entirely different reason, he was stopped.

"Hey, hey. Why the hell are you touching that filthy thing?"

"If that bastard sticks to me, I'm kicking first."

...Seriously.

'I guess it's because I'm the one with the last employee number.'

Let's just let it slide. I was already short on enough calories to waste any on walking…

'Good grief...'

Even in these dire conditions, I could sense a strict hierarchy within the team of “workers.” Or, maybe it’s just because the working conditions are so shitty that it stands out even more.

‘For now, I’d better not draw attention to myself.’

I couldn't forget that today was an exploration mission. Quietly, I fell in line at the very back of the procession.

After walking a little farther, the end of the corridor came into view.

The passage leading to the "Restricted Off-Duty Area" was still sealed shut. It was the very place where I'd first woken up, beside the dried-out ancestral altar.

Instead, the door next to it stood open, revealing a newly accessible area.

"...!"

...It was a spacious indoor section divided into three stalls, almost like a livestock barn.




WORK ASSIGNMENT WAITING AREA



The facility still had the same shabby, rusted atmosphere as before. However, clean signboards, as if they had been added afterward, hung above, made to the same standard as those used in Daydream Inc.

Standing inside each of the three sections were employees dressed in uniforms resembling full-body protective suits.

'Staff members.'

It was the first time I'd seen people here who weren't workers. And the logo and name tags on their uniforms…

They were incredibly familiar.

'They're from the Security Team.'

A Daydream Security name tag.

The instant it entered my field of vision, my thoughts seemed to accelerate on reflex.

'So this really is a facility operated under Daydream Inc.'

The moment it truly sank in, a brief shiver, almost akin to relief, brushed across my chest before disappearing.




[Yes, yes. Get your work assignment from the supervisor.]




The security staff, each carrying a clipboard, approached the workers packed into the different sections like a labor market. They checked attendance in numerical order, starting from the lowest employee numbers.

I quietly took my place in the corner where team Z stood, using the corner of my eye to observe the crowd of workers.

As expected… The ones who looked to be in the best condition were, unsurprisingly, the people in team X.

"Number 491? Yeah... that lunatic? No idea. I don't think she ever came out of her room."

"Did you hear that? Hah, they say she finally lost her mind. That's hilarious."

"Imagine being so scared of ghosts that you go insane~."

Two people from Team X snickered as they mocked the worker from Team Y who hadn't shown up for roll call.

'They're the guys I saw when I first woke up.'

The bastards who dumped water over my head and beat me up beside the ancestral altar.

Let's see, judging by the red stickers attached to their necks… 

'X145. X141.'

They were definitely much earlier employee numbers than my Z999.

Considering what was written in the diary… those numbers are probably among the better ones in this workplace.




— Looks like the workers are divided into X, Y, and Z ranks... and I got put into Z. Judging by the rooms, the earlier the letter in the alphabet, the better your treatment.




'So they're actually proud of having a better rank around here, huh...'

If they end up running into even a single ghost story they can't handle, they'll be dead in an instant, just like the person who used to be in my room.

For a fleeting moment, I almost felt sorry for those people who had no idea they were sprinting barefoot through a minefield full of unexploded bombs. But I quickly came to my senses. They're still obviously better off than me, a Z999...

Besides, anyone who dumps water on someone's head and beats them in the face can't exactly be normal. Forget it.

In any case, I'd gotten a general idea of the workers' condition.

'...The farther back the team, and the higher the employee number, the fewer people there seem to be in each room.'

Looking more closely, I also began to notice people whose condition seemed off… 

One man frantically spun a RumiCube tile rack clutched in both hands, even though half the tiles were missing. Another muttered to himself nonstop. One person's hair looked half-burned away, while another stood nearby barefoot, staring blankly into space.

'...Must be a symptom of contamination.”

Were they receiving treatment? There was no way to know.

Fortunately, I didn't have to wait long for a chance to find out.

"Yeah, yeah... all three people from our room are here."

...That’s because, after the roll call for the room in front of ours finished, a security officer stepped up in front of me.

"......"

Hoping I might recognize some distinctive features, I took a closer look. But the security staff at the Namhae Branch were strangely devoid of any identifying characteristics.

Wrapped head to toe in identical protective suits, they all looked exactly the same.

'They look... like standard-issue models.'

Unlike the Security Team at the headquarters, who carried an eerie, dangerous presence, these ones gave off the unsettling impression of a faceless, inhuman collective.

Looking at the hood covering the security officer's head, I spoke.

"...Hello."

There was no response.

The security officer holding the clipboard glanced at my employee number and, without a hint of emotion, marked an “O” beside Z999.

Then, just above mine, they calmly marked an “X” beside the entry labeled Z993.

Alright, now.

"I think... that person's dead."

The pen stopped.

“Um, since he keeps acting like this… Shouldn't he be sent to a medical room… or something?"

I deliberately spoke in a clumsy, frightened voice, as though I were panicking and blurting out whatever came to mind.

A brief, dry silence followed.

Then the security officer slowly raised his hand and… 

Pointed toward the back of the waiting area.

"......"

When I turned my head, I found a notice for the workers posted on the wall.




Additional purchases of daily necessities and services are available at your own discretion.



(Cash ONLY. No cards.)



What kind of insane nonsense is this...?

‘Don't tell me... even the infirmary counts as a service the workers have to pay for themselves?’

Somebody, seriously, please save me…

What kind of late-stage capitalist hell was this? The working environment itself was straight out of a horror story.

But asking something like, "Do I happen to have any cash on me?" would have looked far too suspicious for someone who was supposed to have lived here for over two weeks.

I'd get a chance to figure it out naturally sooner or later.

"Ah, I understand..."

I mumbled the words under my breath. The security officer simply walked past me with the clipboard in hand, bringing the workers' morning roll call to an end.

And then the long-awaited “work” finally began.

[X, Y, and Z Teams, workers in the 990s, move out together.]

Along with me, roughly a dozen other workers had blindfolds placed over their eyes as we were led somewhere on foot. From there, we were loaded onto a vehicle and transported again.



Whiiiiiiirr— 



The rattling of old machinery and the roar of the engine made it impossible to tell where we were going or even how we were moving.

What was certain was that after about five to ten minutes, we'd arrived somewhere.




[Yes. We’ve confirmed your arrival.]

[Workers, remove your blindfolds and prepare to disembark.]




When I pulled off the blindfold, a rust-covered space came into view.

Only then did I realize I had been riding in something resembling an old mining cart sitting on worn railway tracks.

'Hmm.'

The interior resembled a sealed mine tunnel, or perhaps some kind of detention facility used as a break area. Yet the humidity was strangely high, with droplets of water steadily falling onto the floor.




Drip.




The entire place felt gloomy and worn with age.

'If you kept even harmless ghost stories locked up in a place like this, wouldn't they eventually take a dark turn too…?'

The branch office concept was really solid. I wonder what kind of lunatic designed this place.




[All workers, disembark!]



Anyway, I got off here as well, following the familiar announcement.




[Security personnel, please confirm that all protective gear is worn.]




A moment later, something thick was fastened around my left forearm. It looked somewhere between a blood pressure monitor and a protective brace.

Then it locked itself in place, making it impossible for me to remove it on my own.

'...Phew.'

Around me, I could hear workers with higher employee numbers quietly muttering unverified protective chants, while those with lower numbers laughed and joked among themselves.

Under the watchful eyes of the security staff, I was made to stand in front of a door halfway down a corridor, one that looked completely indistinguishable from all the others.




[Worker Z999, deploying.]




The iron door creaked open with a long, grating groan.

Before I could react, a security officer shoved me straight through the doorway. My weakened body offered no resistance as I stumbled inside.

'Wait a second…'

Are they just throwing me in here without a manual?!



Creeeak, thud.



Before I could even protest, the door slammed shut behind me.

'Come on, please.'

This is driving me crazy, seriously… 

I was trapped.

An instinctive fear welled up inside me.

'From here on, the ghost story begins…'

Tension instinctively tightened around the back of my neck. No, it had simply been far too long since I'd last found myself locked inside a dark, enclosed space with something that was obviously horrifying. I squeezed my eyes shut.

Maybe the only reason I didn't scream was because I didn't even have the strength left for that… 

"...Phew."

No matter what, if I wanted to make it out alive, I had to do my job properly.

Taking a slow, deep breath, I opened my eyes and looked straight ahead.

"......"

The dark interior.

Inside the crude containment room, packed earth covered the floor while rough concrete walls surrounded it.

The air was thick with the smell of dampness. A single red lamp hung overhead, casting its dim glow downward. Its crimson light fell upon an old, weathered chair. And resting there all by itself...

...was a wooden doll.




 — So far, they’ve been making me set fire to a wooden doll with a lighter every day.




Cold sweat ran down my back.

'Damn it...'

This was the very ghost story assigned to the late Z993.




— I’m sorry, please save me. I’m sorry, please save me. I’m sorry, please save me. I’m sorry, please save me. I’m sorry, please save me. I’m sorry, please save me. I’m sorry, please save me. I’m sorry, please save me. I’m sorry, please save me.




The final page of the diary flashed through my mind, and my mouth immediately went dry.

Of all the ones it had to be.

At the very least, one thing had already been proven. Mishandle this Darkness, and you die. The wooden doll, stained with grease, was covered from head to toe in charred burn marks. Every time my eyes landed on it, the contents of the diary resurfaced, making my blood run cold.

'But while I was reading that diary, one thought kept coming back to me...'




[Worker Z999.]




"...!"

I jerked my head up.




[Yes, you can hear me, can't you? Worker Z999. Now, hit that wooden doll.]




“......”

A voice echoed through the room, as though speakers had been installed somewhere out of sight.

It was the detached, indifferent voice of a researcher.

But… 

'...Hit it?'




Up until now, all they'd ever had me do was set the wooden doll on fire with a lighter every day.




Just like with the previous worker, it was obviously the kind of thing anyone would hesitate to do.

'Hah.'

Instead of clearing the ghost story on my own, I was in a position where I had to carry out whatever the researcher ordered. 

And this… had a very high chance of getting me killed!




[Don't look around. Just follow the instructions.]




"...Excuse me."

No reply came.

I remained standing where I was.




[You can stand there all you want. We'll leave you in that room until you do it. You know you don't get paid if you're late, so what's the problem?]




"......Researcher. Are you in th..."




[If you fail to comply with the next instruction, you'll receive a penalty point.]




"......"

I slowly turned my head.

Toward the unsettling wooden doll sitting on the chair.




[Hit it.]




I took one step forward.




[Hurry up.]




Then I raised a hand and struck the doll.
