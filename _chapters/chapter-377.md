# **CHAPTER 377**

The containment room for ghost story Qterw-C-348.

 A red light illuminated a chair that was standing alone in the room.

".......”

I stared straight ahead.

The wooden doll was beneath the chair, hidden within its shadow. 

The ground had been dug up halfway, and the doll's neck and arms were twisted outward, protruding from the soil. 

...I was certain I'd buried it completely beneath the dirt. 

An instinctive fear wells up.

It was worse than when I first saw it. It's because of last night.

‘Phew.’

I wiped the cold sweat from my brow as I looked at the wooden doll, half buried in the soil, just like the ghost I had seen…

'...It looks like it struggled desperately to crawl out, but never quite made it.' 

 As expected, getting covered in dirt was a worthwhile act.

   \[Yes. Just bury it in the soil again.\]

So, I intended to follow the researcher's instructions exactly as given. Though… I did plan to do a little something extra on top of that. 

"......."

As I dug a new hole in the dirt with both hands beneath the chair, I thought about this ghost story.

‘The reason I went out of my way to bury this in the dirt on the very first day was…’

It was because of a certain element mentioned in \<Dark Exploration Records\>, but missing from the doll right in front of me.

   **Each instance can be identified as Qterw-C-2554 by the talisman attached to it.**

The talisman.

More specifically...  The inscription written on it.

   **The talisman was severely damaged and covered in markings. Upon restoring and interpreting the inscription, it was confirmed that it contained phrases used in a Sumang-gut**¹ **ritual for a drowning victim.**

It was an important hint. 

'The fact that the talisman was attached in a damaged state means... it either couldn't function properly, or it had become corrupted and produced an unintended effect.' 

A broken talisman. It was a cliché of ghost stories. 

I actually experienced a similar situation at a certain high school myself.

To put it another way…

‘Usually, the way to break a curse is to return the talisman to its original state and perform the ritual.’ 

But if I did that, the darkness would be terminated.

‘That's the Supernatural Disaster Management Agency's way. It's not the way of Daydream Inc.’

This company ‘manages and operates’ the darkness.

‘Even if they had guessed as much, they probably wouldn't have tried it.’

They hadn't removed the talisman for no reason.

They were undoubtedly searching for a way to let their employees clear it without bringing about its termination.

In that case, what I had to do was simple.

'I just need to figure out the Daydream's way of clearing it.' 

Find cases of people who survived for a relatively long time after coming into contact with the doll, or the rare few who survived altogether.

The problem was that more than half of those cases were either far too personal or lacked enough context to be useful.

For example, cases like this.

**Visited a Buddhist temple after coming into contact with the doll. Two days later, take refuge as a Buddhist nun.**

**It was later confirmed that** **there is no water element** **in the explorer’s horoscope.**

I couldn’t do anything about this.

‘It's not like I can change my horoscope, nor is it a situation where I can become a Buddhist nun.’

In the first place, dolls are ghost stories that appear in various forms that aren’t standardized. They’re designed to instill fear through its ambiguity—it’s hard to discern any patterns, and they only reveal certain tendencies.

So I’d narrow it down.

‘I’m going to categorize the investigation records.’

So I’m going to find one category with the clearest pattern for me.

The one I’ve already identified.

**Confirmed that the words for the ‘Sumang-gut’ for those who have drowned are written here.**

Sumang-gut\!

'You just need to pay close attention to the case descriptions where something similar to a Gut ritual was performed.'

Acts that touch a similar context to some extent, although the ghost story is not closed. 

Of course, since I’m not a specialized agent with the Supernatural Disaster Management Agency like some people are, I can’t know all the specific procedures or methods.

But the important thing isn’t the minute details, but rather the core elements.

So I narrowed it down to two points.

First.

Bury the doll in the dirt, just as it was.

**Exploration Records \#5**

**A civilian (20s, male) who found a doll on the Pohang ▇ ▇ coast experienced hallucinations after posting an acquisition on the Internet.**

**After visiting the shaman, the doll was buried back where it was, as advised.**

**Found dead after 41 days. Found mud and water, and ▇ ▇ under the bed of the dead, around the mattress.**

Burying it in the soil dramatically slows the passage of time.

‘Maybe it's not important to return it to the original state, and the process itself seems to have been a kind of exorcism.’

It was the context that carried significance. 

That was why, on the first day, I had imitated that situation without hesitating, just to buy myself time. 

And then...

‘Find the ritual.’

**Perform a ritual with the doll.**

Exploration records with that phrase. 

‘A lot of people must have hoped that shamanic rituals or other folk practices might actually work.’ 

Even if they couldn't decipher the talisman, it was only natural for people to seek out a mudang or a Buddhist temple first.

‘It's similar to what a person might do after seeing hallucinations of ghosts.’

They burned paper money, recited incantations, consulted fortune-tellers, performed ancestral rites, burned incense, built graves, and poured alcohol on them.

But only a few of them worked.

To be specific...

Seeking a fortune.

Performing ancestral rites.

Building a grave and pouring alcohol on it.

These are the three.

And these three have something in common.

Have you noticed?

‘Food.’

When consulting a fortune-teller, people throw rice.

When performing ancestral rites, they set up an offering table.

The wine poured onto a grave is also considered food.

'Which means, the most essential part of the Sumang-gut described on the talisman is…’

Offering food to the dead.

That was why I'd prepared these.

Rice and alcohol.

“...... ”

I took out the instant rice and the can of beer I'd bought from the snack bar yesterday.

Then I opened the lid of the instant rice and placed it in front of the hole I had dug earlier.

After taking a deep breath.

“...... ”

I reached beneath the chair...

...and picked up the doll that had been half buried in the ground.

The doll was fine.

All the joints I broke have been put back together; it looks as if nothing ever happened. But I know.

The wooden doll remembered me.

No…

The drowned man.

*Ha—*

…I could hear my breath. 

Goosebumps broke out on my neck.

*I-I-Na-Ta-Ae-Jo-Eun-Mi-Seo-Geu-Ri-Pa*

Ignoring it, I moved the wooden doll. My hands were trembling. *Thud*. I lowered the doll into the freshly dug hole, then covered it with soil once more. The dirt slipped through my fingers as my hands shook. I hurriedly shoveled more earth over it. Finally, the moment the doll disappeared completely beneath the soil...

‘Okay…’

*Drip.*

A cold drop of water landed on the top of my head.

A face was hanging from the ceiling above me.

*Haiineotaaejoeunmiseugeojeuripadoie.*

*Drip. Drip. Drip,*

Moisture dripped onto my face. The fishy smell of water filled the air. Its mutilated face opened up, as arms stretched out from both sides to grab me. 

Its mouth moved.

*Neotaae Jo-eun Miseu Geoju-ri Pa*

I looked. 

***Haiineotaaejoeunmiseugeojeuripadoie Haiineotaaejoeunmiseugeojeuripadoie Haiineotaaejoeunmiseugeojeuripadoie Haiineotaaejoeunmiseugeojeuripadoie Haiineotaaejoeunmiseugeojeuripadoie Haiineotaaejoeunmiseugeojeuripadoie Haiineotaaejoeunmiseugeojeuripadoie Haiineotaaejoeunmiseugeojeuripadoie Haiineotaaejoeunmiseugeojeuripadoie Haiineotaaejoeunmiseugeojeuripadoie Haiineotaaejoeunmiseugeojeuripadoie Haiineotaaejoeunmiseugeojeuripadoie***

Ah.

*Ha—*

I shouldn't have understood.

I should have understood...\!

“Ugh,”

Something salty was pouring out of my mouth.

“Wow.”

Mud. That steaming, squelching pile of mud beneath the salty beach mixed into my saliva and poured down. No, wait—was it saliva, or was it breath? Or was it not breath, but mucus? 

***Come closer***

“Eeeek,”

The agony, terror, cold, and loneliness of drowning made my face go pale. It was choking me.

I couldn’t lower my head; I was trapped. I barely managed to pull the beer can from my chest. My hand were trembling as if I were floundering in the water; the can, the can…

I opened it.

*Fizz.*

With a short sound, the foam from the beer that was shaken to the limit spilled over my fingers and flowed down.

*Quick.* 

My vision was blurry. A face covered in flesh and hair touches my forehead. I couldn’t look down; my hands were all...

*I wish I were dead.*

I tipped the can upside down.

“......”

*Splash.*

I heard the sound of something flowing.

The sound of a liquid—not water—pouring down onto the spot where I buried the doll.

The alcohol. 

“.......”

I lowered my head.

The drowning victim, whose head had been hanging from the ceiling, was gone.

All that remained was the interior of the isolation room, lit by a red light.

A chair standing alone.

And an empty instant rice container rolling around beneath it. That was all.

....

....

“Huh,”

That was it.

‘I almost died…’

I almost choked to death from sheer terror.

I forced myself to stand up, even though my body was about to collapse to the floor.

My legs were shaking. 

"It's been so long... The sensation of escaping from this hell..." 

\[...Worker Z999?\]

"Yes."

Right, you couldn’t hear me.

\[Worker Z999, can you wave at the door?\]

I was willing to do so. 

\[No….\]

A faint note of astonishment crept into the researcher’s voice.

The damp, eerie atmosphere that had been weighing down on my skin in the isolation room had vanished before I knew it, and I…

Was fine.

\[Hey, come out first.\]

You’re more than welcome.

“Phew.”

I took a deep breath and headed toward the open isolation room door.

\[Hey, don’t move\! Stay still.\]

As soon as I stepped out of the isolation room, a security guard arrived at the researcher’s instruction and immediately took the armband off my forearm.

‘Okay.’

Even after that, no one ever told me what was inside or what the results were.

But I knew.

‘What I did definitely meets the standards of the headquarters field exploration team.’

This was clearly a different league from the other workers who simply spent a few hours in the morning dabbling in ghost stories before heading out.

‘I must have gotten a C-grade.’

I was also certain that this is a grade that was nearly impossible to achieve by chance among the workers at the branch office—the ones who are treated like disposable tools and given no information whatsoever.

I was certain of it.

And so, that afternoon.

\[Worker Z999, you’re being called.\]

Before I had even finished lunch, I was summoned separately and accompanied by a security officer, and was subjected to relentless questioning by a researcher in a room labelled “Work Communication Room.”

They were mostly questions asked by someone desperate to create a standard operating procedure.

To statements that could be summarized as “Why on earth did you do that?” 

I gave simple, straightforward answers.

Just because.

“Um… I thought it looked hungry, so I shared some food…”

\[…So you gave the wooden doll some instant rice, is that what happened?\]

“Yeah...”

There’s no reason.

It was just, “I was hungry, so I figured this one must be hungry too.”

When I told the researcher that I’d seen a ghost who looked hungry, I heard him grind his teeth, and then he fired off a barrage of persistent follow-up questions.

\[Why did you bring beer?\]

"I had to pay for the water. They told me to pay it twice."

\[What?\]

I recounted the story of the two workers who had threatened me in a tone that betrayed no trace of intelligence.

“It seemed like everyone liked beer... and I thought the doll would like it, too.”

\[……\]

Right.

I’m going to stick firmly to the concept that “none of this was intentional.”

Just as if the actions of someone in a state of mental or physical weakness had somehow fallen into place by chance.

And this was also a topic that was quite challenging yet fascinating for a ghost story researcher to tackle.

\[So… You weren’t really sure, but it all worked out, right?\]

“Huh? Yeah...”

It was luck. 

It was a factor that’s hard to measure, so people tended to handle it with care.

Some might argue that, scientifically speaking, it’s nothing more than a statistical phenomenon, but in the world of ghost stories, it’s significant.

No, it might even be the most essential talent of all.

‘It’s the lucky ones who have the most survival stories.’

I often found myself in situations I couldn’t logically comprehend where I had to rely solely on luck to navigate them.

And those particularly lucky exploration cases are always a staple subject in the Darkness Exploration Records.

‘They’ll definitely want to study this.’

Even if the researcher whose voice I heard was the type who has no real interest in research and was fixated only on the points, that was fine.

‘They’ll probably want to put me here and there to see if they can extract more high-grade dream essence.’

That means they’d want to use me for a little longer, and it means they’d want to take care of me just a little bit better.

Let’s give them a little nudge in that direction now.

“Ugh…”

I also made a point of emphasizing that my physical condition was absolutely terrible.

\[If you’re not feeling well, go to the infirmary.\]

“I can’t spend money… I need to save up…”

\[Sigh…\]

Feeling a surge of frustration and irritation, the researcher let out a sigh.

‘But ‘autonomy’ also means that if you refuse, that’s the end of it.’

But he probably wouldn’t want to pay for it himself either.

'Judging by the researcher’s attitude, I’ll probably have to cover it out of my own pocket.’

You couldn’t expect that from a ‘Daydream researcher’ character type.

Instead, another option naturally would come to mind.

For example... a way to maintain this “promising” worker’s physical condition so they could keep working—without having to spend any money, all while making them believe it was their own idea.

\[Just go ahead. Worker Z999.\]

“Huh...?”

\[A good thing will happen later, so don't forget who you owe it to.\] 

And so, that very evening.

“Uh...?”

“A-a special transfer?”

I escaped Z999.

\*\*\*\*

Dinner time.

Just before we entered the cafeteria, a notice was posted in the work area.

Special Reassignment  
Z999 \-\> Y492

It was just a slapdash printout on A4 paper, but the impact it had on me was undeniable.

“Number Z999?”

“That’s the very last number.”

“Who is it?”

“You know, that weird bird that looks like it’d collapse if you hit it even once… Eek\!”

He’s right here.

The whispers—things like, “Wasn’t he already dead?”—stopped instantly. 

And I felt eyes on me, watching me as if I were a specimen.

That distinctive gaze—as if they were looking at something at the very bottom of the social hierarchy, whether it was mocking, wanting to stay out of it, or just looking for an easy target to take their frustration out on—shifted ever so slightly.

— How did he do it? 

I guess I could sum it up like this.

Doubt and a bit of confusion.

‘It feels like trouble’s coming.’

The moment those guys came to their senses, they'd probably start looking around for anything else they could squeeze out of me. 

‘Dealing with those two X-ranks is already more than enough... ’

Since my whole act was to play the easy target before they actually decided I was one, I grabbed my meal and got out of there as fast as I could. 

And, somewhere along the way, I found myself unexpectedly touched.

‘Wow, this is crazy.’

Starting from Y-rank, they even offered a proper takeout service for meals\! 

“Y492...”

Better yet, the menu itself was enough to move me. 

‘It's tonkatsu, isn't it…’

The piping-hot fried pork came with steamed rice and radish kimchi.

Compared to the days of cold rice and a few measly anchovies, this was an honest-to-goodness meal. I was practically on the verge of tears. 

Sure, the tonkatsu looked like one of those pre-made frozen cutlets you'd get at a highway rest stop, but who cared? 

I carefully took my meal and dashed to the break room.

Before the others who had been eyeing me could follow, I quickly slipped inside. 

“Phew.”

A success, for now.

For the record, the sticker on my neck wasn’t replaced during this process.

‘I guess since they just slapped me into the vacant spot in Team Y on the spot, they didn’t get around to updating that part yet.’

— Once this week's shift is over, there will be another official reassignment, and if you work even harder and do a good job, you might end up in a better place.

Thinking of those subtle words, the researcher seemed to be secretly hoping for the original intended effect. To encourage a proactive work ethic.

‘Sorry, but now that a livable environment has been created, I’m just going to stick it out for two weeks.’

No, I don’t really feel sorry at all. A research position that’s just a pipe dream… and his true colors are pretty obvious.

I fell silent, thinking of a certain researcher at headquarters.

Anyway, I felt a little excited knowing that a decent bedroom was waiting for me.

And the freshly packaged, piping-hot tonkatsu even more so\!

“Let’s pack things up quickly.”

I quickly gathered my belongings from Z999's quarters, the worst workers' break room in the facility.

There wasn't much to pack, anyway.  
I didn't own much to begin with.

'I'd better take the journal, just in case.'

I picked up the notebook left behind by No. 993, along with the candy, a few pieces of chocolate, and the bottle of water I'd kept beside my mattress.

Just as I lifted the mattress slightly—

“…Huh?”

Rip. The edge of the mattress, soaked in muddy water, tore.

“...\!”

It seemed as if something had caught on a protruding part of the floor.

‘What is it?’

I immediately checked under the mattress.

Perhaps because a ghost had grabbed my foot last night and made me move it, or because the mattress was wet, the floor right underneath was scraped away.

And beneath that, where there used to be concrete, there were patches of dirt scattered here and there.

'...Has the concrete turned into dirt?' 

It certainly looked as if part of the floor here had turned to dirt because of the urban legend about the water ghost doll. I shuddered.

But something glinted faintly in that dirt.

‘…A handle?’

I brushed off a little more dirt.

The outline that became clearly visible looked like part of a doorknob or a safe handle.

'Was it embedded in the concrete?' 

Aware of the security cameras likely in this room, I dug it out of the dirt, pretending to be straightening the mattress.

The fragment of the handle dangled loosely in my hand, as if it were trash.

“Hmm.”

This... feels like the pottery shards or screw fragments you sometimes find when digging in the dirt at an old playground.

But it felt strange to just throw it away.

Something like this was embedded in the concrete?

And right here at the Namhae Branch, no less.

I recalled the layout of this place, which felt like a closed-off mine.

Daydream Inc. went out of its way to build a branch office and a quarantine facility right here.

It seems highly likely that this place itself holds some kind of… supernatural significance. 

But the fact that a piece of a doorknob—or something like that—was hidden on the floor is, no matter how I look at it, deeply significant.

Doesn’t it seem like a symbol of a way to escape somewhere?

Or perhaps to store something.

“Hmm.”

I took a closer look at the piece I’d dug up.

No unusual characteristics in the material, no strange inscriptions, no bloodstains, no sudden panic or urge to kill as I held it... Nothing like that.

“Okay.”

First hurdle cleared. Let’s just take care of this for now.

I quietly slipped the piece of the doorknob into my work pants pocket. I didn’t forget to resolve to toss it out immediately if anything seemed off.

And just like that, the moving preparations were complete.

‘Let’s move into the dorm and grab some food first… \! ‘

I wondered if there might be someone trying to cause trouble at the Y490 dorm as well, but at this hour, everyone was probably at the cafeteria.

“Alright.”

I picked up my belongings, glanced outside, confirmed there were no other workers around, and moved out immediately.

And then, into the room at Y490… I finally entered.

*Click.*

As I opened the door and stepped inside, I saw a surprisingly spacious room.

‘Wow.’

It looked a bit run-down, like an older apartment building, but there was a living room with vinyl flooring and two bedrooms.

It seemed the rooms were divided by gender. I could even see locks on the doors...\!

‘I’m tearing up.’

Now it finally feels like a place where people live.

I’m on the verge of feeling a small sense of happiness just knowing that the living spaces are separate.

“...\!”

The moment I looked under the worn-out sofa in the living room, I froze.

Someone was lying there.

A worker with messy, disheveled black hair.

‘Surely not another dead body...??’

Wow, please.

But thankfully, they moved.

‘Phew...’

My sigh of relief was short-lived, as I realized the person’s condition was far from normal.

The person, dressed in shabby work clothes, looked absolutely wretched, gasping for breath while scraping the floor with both hands.

Several fingernails stretched toward the door were already broken. 

Their palms were covered in scrapes and calluses.

‘Who is this?’

Wait, was there actually someone still in the dorm at this hour? That’s strange…

‘...Hold on a second.’

I recalled something I’d heard yesterday morning at the workers’ waiting area.

— No. 491? Oh, that crazy kid? I don’t know. She hasn’t come out of her room.

“.......”

They were Team Y, right?

‘No, surely not.’

So that’s the person.

I went through all the trouble of switching rooms, only to end up with a roommate who’s already lost their mind due to the side effects of the horror story…

'Sigh......'

No, I was prepared for this.

‘Just don’t provoke them and brush it off.’ 

Still, if we’re alone together, they might try to harm me, so I’d better be careful.

I stepped back and quietly left the room, deciding to go back to Z999’s room, grab a bite to eat, and then return.

And then I turned around….

…….

‘I’ll just check on them.’

They’re not dead yet, after all.

It couldn’t hurt me as their roommate if I could improve their condition, right?

Besides, just leaving them alone might actually be dangerous.

‘Phew.’

I held back a sigh and cautiously approached Y491 (presumably) in the living room.

“Hey, are you o—”

I couldn’t finish the sentence, “Are you okay?”

“...\!\!”

….Because I realized something.

That this person looked familiar.

But, but…..

‘There’s no way this person should be here–’

In a state of shock and terror, I cautiously moved closer to confirm the person’s profile… 

......

It was indeed someone I knew.

‘Oh, my God.’

Assistant Manager Jin Nasol.

A full-time employee of Daydream Inc. Headquarters’ Field Investigation Team.

The former elite member of Team A was right here.
