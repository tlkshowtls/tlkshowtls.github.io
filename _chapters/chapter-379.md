# **CHAPTER 379**

It was surprisingly common on the wiki to find that Daydream Inc. puts dozens or hundreds of people into a single ghost story at the same time.

Because it's fun.

I even experienced this firsthand not long after I joined Daydream Inc.

It was a place where dozens of employees had died.

‘The Manor of the Blind…’

Just thinking about that mansion, where a bizarre exhibition was held, and people were forced to pay with their facial features and limbs as admission, still makes my mouth go dry.

\[Just a reminder, we’ll be starting special night work today.\]

That's when I more or less figured it out. The moment they started gathering over a hundred workers and moving them that evening. My PTSD alarm was practically blaring inside my head.

‘This is it, one hundred percent.’

They were planning to throw every last one of these workers into a single ghost story. And exploration records like this usually cut the survival rate in half.

Because those were the most entertaining to read. 

‘Damn it.'

I forced my clenched expression to relax.

Looking over at Jin Nasol, I saw that she seemed to have reached the same conclusion. Her gaze had gone cold as she quietly gathered up some painkillers.

As expected of the elite team...

"This stuff is weak as hell. Is this seriously all we've got?"

"...Yes."

I don't know anymore.

'Let's not have that much faith in her...'

In this world of ghost stories, where there’s no mercy, no compassion, and no sense of reason, it’s best for my own safety to trust only myself. After all, even I might betray myself…

“Damn it, I need to get some rest so I can work tomorrow. What the hell are they doing, calling people out like this in the middle of the night?”

“Wait, no way, they're saying they'll pay us double with the night shift premium...\!”

“S-seriously?”

“Holy fuck, that corporation has some nerve\!”

Two hundred in cash.

Faced with that magic word, the workers grumbled but didn’t put up a fight.

\[Yeah, yeah. Put on your blindfolds.\]

As I was being carried away like a piece of luggage, I desperately tried to figure it out and narrow down the possibilities.

What kind of ghost story could this be?

\[Get off.\]

Soon, the minecart came to a stop, and we were led to a single door.

\[Alright, line up. Yes. Line up in groups of five.\]

Groups of five, then.

'That’s an odd number.'

Seeing that they were dividing us into random groups based on the order we lined up, I calculated the situation and positioned myself roughly in the middle of the line.

I debated whether to split up with Assistant Manager Jin Nasol, but figured that even in the worst-case scenario, where only some members of a group survived, the two of us could still help each other, so I stood with her.

‘It’s usually better to stick together.’

\[Uh… Yeah. This works.\]

After the security guards checked the workers and exchanged some silent signals,

The isolation room finally opened.

*Squeeeek…*

“......”

“Isn't it too dark in there?” 

I couldn't see inside the door.

The place, shrouded in thick darkness, even felt like a kind of void.

“Hey, you'd have to give us a flashlight or something before we can decide whether to go in or not.”

“And can all these people even fit in there?”

\[Yes. It’s spacious enough inside. Once you go in, you’ll be able to see everything.\]

“Still….”

\[Ugh... Seriously.\]

Irritation began to seep into the researcher’s annoyed voice.

\[Then skip the assignment. Go ahead and skip it—and get a penalty.\]

“...\!”

\[So either deal with the consequences, or get in there quickly. Can’t you see that you’re wasting everyone’s time right now? Do you all want to lose sleep tonight?\]

“That..."

"Yeah, you're a grown adult and you're throwing a fit because it's dark?”

“Just go inside, that's all.” 

Then, as a few workers with lower numbers chimed in and gave him a look, the worker who had been protesting fell silent.

Even though it was actually a request they needed themselves, they were instinctively trying not to upset the researcher.

'Most of the workers... seemed to think that anyone who didn't come back after a job had either been fired or sent to the hospital.' 

They either dismissed the rumors that the back-numbered workers had died.

Or, if they happened to believe in that sort of thing, assumed those people had simply been spiritually weak and fallen victim to ghosts.

And since they themselves had done the exact same work without anything happening, they believed they'd continue to be fine.

‘Complacent, but realistic.’

On the contrary, I could see anxiety and tension etched on the faces of the workers in the Z900 series. Some were stiff with fear, while others already looked as though they were only half present. 

\[Pick up your instructions and enter. Everyone in the same line may enter at the same time.\]

But the entry had already begun.

The workers at the front entered the darkness one by one.

Then it was my turn in line.

“........”

The security guard, who was standing closest to me, handed me a piece of paper.

Was this the same person as on the first day?

“T-thank you...” 

I took it.

The moment I stepped through the isolation room door, holding a slightly shabby piece of paper… 

Thud.

Darkness engulfed us.

"......”

Strangely enough, we didn’t bump into the team ahead of us.

We slowly made our way forward.

One step.

Another step.

“Wait, just how far do we have to go...?” 

Just as the worker next to me finished speaking…

*Whoosh*.

I heard a brief gust of wind.

My view cleared.

“...\!”

A mountain breeze tickled my forehead.

Before my eyes, a slope on a wild mountain covered in dark blue tree branches came into view.

“A... mountain?”

“You mean this was a door leading outside? No way, how can this be?”

“No, I mean, on a random hill like this…?” 

Assistant Manager Jin Nasol gestured with her chin.

A piece of paper in my hand.

‘Check it.’

As it happened, I was already unfolding the piece of paper I’d been given.

If simply entering the isolation room was the trigger to get in, then this was most likely the clue to escaping.

The contents of the folded paper came into view.

### **A Thrilling Test of Courage**

#### **Collect ~~4~~ *3* stamps and claim your prize.**

*Let’s visit the famous spots on Mount Saegyeong in the darkness of night to build our courage and self-discipline and strengthen our minds.*

**Fairy Rock – Saegyeong Mountain Lodge – Osaek Mineral Spring**

|  Stamp  |  |  |  |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |

Prize Pickup Location:  
Information Desk in front of the entrance on the 1st floor of the Training Center

“…A test of courage?”

Excuse me.

For heaven’s sake, couldn’t they at least pretend it wasn’t a ghost story?

‘Why are they acting so earnest…’

A “courage test” ghost story, of all things.

“Hey, let’s take a look at that together.” 

I obediently and quickly held out the paper so my teammates could see it clearly.

A phrase at the very bottom of the old-fashioned, tacky, and slightly crude notice which looked like it had been made in Photoshop, caught my eye anew.

This experience is available at all times

For parties renting facilities at our Shinmaru Retreat Center.

No registration required, you can participate anytime.

At that moment.

“Shinmaru Retreat Center?”

…\!

"Do you know this place?"

"Uh, uh… isn't this the training center where all the kids died after that accident?"

What?

Chills are sweeping over me, as if cold water has poured down my back. 

No, I’ve heard about it before.

“Not long before I started working here… you know, a whole bunch of students died.”

“......”

“I heard that place was some kind of training center run by a cult. Didn't they say they couldn't even find the kids' bodies?”

An eerie silence fell.

“…But we’re actually here right now? Oh, man.”

“No, wait, let’s go back and ask the person in charge of the internal broadcast, then come back...”

I turned around.

I could see a dimly lit path through the woods.

The door to the isolation room was nowhere in sight.

We were all alone in the middle of the woods.

“.......”

“Huh, what's going on...?”

“Wait, the door we just came in through... No.”

This isn't going to work.

It was clear that these people had never before experienced such a raw, unadulterated darkness firsthand.

“Is it too dark to see?”

“I guess so? If it's not that, then...”

“Ah, that must be it. I mean, I can't really see clearly...”

I quickly chimed in.

I felt like if I didn't, everyone would start panicking.

'Hurry up and figure out an escape route.'

First, I quickly tried to figure out what kind of ghost story this was. So... a training center where people died, a courage test...

'This is such a cliché.'

Once again, the isolated circumstances were just the kind of common scenario that was perfect for ghost stories. A few typical ghost stories come to mind, but it’s hard to say for sure. Just like with the wooden doll, it could simply be one of many such cases.

‘I feel like if I just experienced one decisive twist, I’d know right away…’

*Tap, tap.*

Assistant Manager Jin Nasol tapped me on the back.

“Leave them behind and let’s move.”

“Huh?”

“The instructions don’t mention anything about the number of people, do they? I said, let's go. Why are we lugging around all this baggage for no reason?”

“J- just a second.”

I’m telling you, on this terrifying hillside, five people feel way safer than just two...\!

‘If we’re going to do this, we might as well go all out. The more of us there are, the less scary it is\!’

I was sweating bullets, trying to come up with a rational explanation for that.

“Damn it, I can’t see a thing? They said we’d see everything once we went inside, but I can’t see a damn thing....”

“Everyone.”

One of the workers chuckled and pulled something out. It was…

“A flashlight\!”

“...\!”

“This is all about outsmarting the problem, isn't it?” 

With a look of tension yet a hint of pride, the worker held up a small flashlight.

“Since it's night work, I made sure to bring this along.”

“Wow, this is why people say you have to be smart.”

“Phew, thanks to this, I’ll be able to get around comfortably. Keep this a secret from the company, though— of course\!” 

The sense of relief provided by light and a clear field of vision returning made everyone’s faces brighten a little.

That’s right. Even in the middle of the night, once there’s light in the mountains, the sense of fear does drop by more than half.

But this is…

‘....Did Daydream not know he was sneaking in like that?’

No, of course they knew. With the CCTV, there’s no way they wouldn’t have caught him.

If they knew, why didn’t they stop him?

And yet, they still refused to give him a flashlight.

   — Once you go inside, you'll see everything there is to see.

That refusal clearly seemed intentional. There must be a reason the researcher wouldn’t give it to me...

“......”

Wait a minute.

“Come on, turn it on quickly...”

“Don’t turn it on.”

“Huh?”

“The flashlight, I told you not to turn it on.”

“I guess you're the type who acts cool when you're scared?”

But the workers who knew me back when I was at Z999 just ignored me, chuckling and going about their business as if nothing were wrong.

“Whatever. You must be scared you might see a ghost for real.”

Damn it.

“Come on, there are no ghosts\~.” 

The worker turned on his flashlight…

Thwack\!

I kicked the opposing worker aside and pinned him to the ground.

“Uh, uuuuh\!”

As I pinned him down, I knocked the flashlight out of his hand.

“Is this guy crazy?\!”

“Shit\!”

The workers rushed at me, trying to tear me away.

‘Damn it\!’

The flashlight rolled away.

Jin Nasol raised one eyebrow as she took in the situation, then approached. It looked like she was going to subdue them, but…

I shouted.

“Close your eyes\!” 

It was already too late.

“Hey\!”

The flashlight, which had slipped from the struggling worker’s hand, was already pressed down.

*Clink*.

The light scattered as it rolled across the floor.

*Clink, clatter*...

Damn it.

“Close your eyes...\!”

“Ugh,”

I snapped at the worker lying beneath me, then pressed his face down, buried my head in the floor, and closed my eyes.

“What the hell? Are you crazy?”

“Why are you telling me not to look? It’s bright and nice out here.”

“Exactly.”

“Wow\~ It’s really bright out here, even in the middle of the night.”

“I can see everything.”

“It's great.”

“Great.”

“Gre-.”

  “Aaaaaaaaaaaah.”

  “Uuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuh, y-y-yeah, it’s gr- great, greaaaattttttt, it's great\!”

“Wow…...”

 “......”

“......”

“I was in the mountains.”

Silence.

……

……..

Now, only the sound of the wind could be heard.

The hands that were trying to tear me apart have vanished without a trace.

A muffled voice comes from beneath me.

“Uh, excuse me.”

……

“Just now, what just happened...”

“Don't open your eyes.”

Huh.

“Assistant Manager. Are you there?”

“Why wouldn’t I be?”

It seems she followed my instructions right away.

'Just as I expected from an elite...'

The next moment, I staggered to my feet.

Then, slowly, I took a few steps... and began walking down the narrow path.

Toward the light beyond my eyelids.

“........”

My legs were shaking. But I didn’t stop.

I don't know how many times I'd gone back and forth toward the brightest spot, where the light pierced even through my eyelids. 

*Thud*. Something kicked against my foot.

“........”

I bent down, picked up the flashlight with sweat-slick hands, and switched it off. 

*Click*.

Has there ever been a time where I was glad to see darkness so much?

‘Phew.’

Only after I put the flashlight in my work pants pocket did I slowly, very slowly, open my eyes.

I saw a dark mountain path.

That was all.

“...You can open your eyes now.” 

As I turned my head, drenched in cold sweat, I saw the worker I had subdued and Assistant Manager Jin Nasol, who had somehow picked up the guidebook and started reading it again.

Two people.

That was all there was to it, too.

"… Seonggil, no, what about the others…?”

“I don’t know.”

I wiped the cold sweat from my brow.

“I can’t see them.”

Right.

I remember.

‘There’s no way I could not know.’ 

This was a ghost story where you’re not supposed to turn on your flashlight.

**\==========================**

**Darkness Exploration Records / Ghost Story**

**\[Let There Be No Light\]**

**: A ghost story featured in \<Darkness Exploration Records\>. Daydream Inc’s identification code is Qterw-C-1010.**

**A ghost story where the horror game gimmick of players getting attacked the moment the lights go out works in the exact opposite way.**

**Anyone caught up in this ghost story goes missing when they turn on the lights, be it a candle, an electric lamp, or a glow stick, while exploring.**

**The scariest part is that not only the person who turned on the light, but also everyone who looked at what was illuminated by that light, goes missing.**

**It remains unclear exactly what the explorers see or what kind of experience they undergo.**

**Explorers who enter the story find themselves participating in a test of courage held at an abandoned retreat center on the outskirts of South Gyeongsang Province, and they cannot escape the ghost story until they have successfully completed the test.**

**\==========================**

I'm going crazy.

‘So… they really did leave us the flashlight on purpose.’

They left it there because they wanted to have a control team to see what would happen if they took it.

And the result?

‘As soon as we entered, two out of five people in this team went missing…’

With no way of knowing what they saw or what even happened.

“......”

It had been a while.

It had been a while since I’d had that experience, where two people who were alive and talking just a moment ago made a strange sound and then vanished.

“Uh, uuuuh. Um,”

“Take a deep breath.” 

But it wasn’t the first time.

“I’ll keep this with me.”

Calm down.

Wiping the sweat from my brow, I removed the batteries from the flashlight in my pocket and put them back in.

The worker didn’t ask for it back.

Instead, he asked a question.

After all, when you see someone who ‘seems to know what’s going on’ amid the shock and panic, you instinctively turn to them for help.

“A-are you saying I shouldn’t turn on the light…?”

“Yes.”

“Why…??”

For reference, Assistant Manager Jin Nasol didn’t ask how I had figured it out.

That’s because she had already figured it out herself.

“You figured it out by reverse deduction because they didn’t give you a flashlight, right?”

“Yes.”

“You’re still sharp.” 

After reading the notice carefully once more, Jin Nasol started walking.

“Well, let’s go, then.”

“Uh, uh, uh, to find the people…”

“Meaning we should go get our stamps. After all, that's the night shift task listed on the paper that the company gave us.”

Before Assistant Manager Jin Nasol could once again suggest that they just leave the worker behind because he was a nuisance, I quickly answered for her.

“And...”

Ha.

After closing my eyes tightly for a moment, I struggled to keep my voice steady as I said,

“Yes. As you said, Assistant Manager, we should move quickly.” 

But I knew.

There was one more condition necessary for this ghost story to hold true.

‘There has to be a situation where you simply can’t bear it without turning on the light...’

Isn’t that obvious?

Otherwise, it would just become one of those ghost stories where you half-heartedly adjust to the darkness and somehow stumble forward slowly, and that’s all it takes to pass.

‘That’s not worth writing up as an exploration case.’

For it to be entertaining, it has to be terrifying and spine-chilling.

I swallowed hard.

‘I just have to… endure it somehow.’

I just have to endure it, thinking of it as just a night of horror and torture.

And this time, there’s an elite from the elite team walking ahead of me, someone who won’t give up easily.

That, at least, was a real comfort.

“…Let’s go.”

So I gritted my teeth and took a step forward.

Into the darkness.
